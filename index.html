Critical Product Analysis — Issue XVII · By Kartik Garg

I tear down products to understand what they got right, what they got wrong, and what I'd enchant differently.

PERPLEXITY AI — The Citation Trust Hex
When "I Trust This" Becomes the Product
Perplexity is the most interesting AI product of 2024 — but it has a trust hex nobody's breaking. It cites sources, yet 60% of users never click them. The real issue: the UI treats citations as footnotes, not confidence signals. I'd redesign the answer card to show source credibility inline, turning "trust me" into "here's why you can trust this." The hex isn't in the model — it's in the design.
AI TrustSearch UXHallucination Risk
NOTION AI — The Co-pilot Positioning Curse
Why a $10 Add-On Feels Like a Detention
Notion AI is a $10/month add-on sitting inside a tool people already pay for — and it shows. Usage drops after week 2 because AI is a feature, not a workflow. The fix isn't better prompts — it's proactive AI that surfaces suggestions without being asked. Think: "You have 3 meeting notes with no action items. Want me to extract them?" That's a habit-forming product, not a chatbox. The curse is reactivity.
AI ProductHabit DesignRetention
LINKEDIN — The Enchanted Reply Trap
When AI Suggestions Make You Feel Like a Bot
LinkedIn's AI reply suggestions are generic enough to be useless and specific enough to feel creepy. The product problem: it optimizes for engagement, not authentic connection. The fix isn't better prompts — it's rethinking when AI should suggest vs. stay silent. The enchantment backfired: it made the platform feel less human, not more useful.
AI ProductSocial TrustEngagement
YOUTUBE MUSIC — The Silencing Charm on Discovery
Best Catalogue, Worst Discovery — A Contradiction
YouTube Music has the best catalogue and the worst discovery. The recommendation engine pushes familiar artists 80% of the time — killing serendipity. I'd redesign the home tab with a "Discover" mode that surfaces genre-adjacent artists, not just more of what you already play. The Silencing Charm was cast on the very thing that makes music magical: surprise.
